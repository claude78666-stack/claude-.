# Kids educational channel: research and name ideas

Plan for an informative kids' YouTube channel with a matching Instagram account. Written 2026-10-10.

## 1. What the top kids' channels do

| Channel | What it does well | What to copy |
|---|---|---|
| **Ms Rachel** | She talks straight to the camera, speaks slowly and clearly, and repeats things. Each song comes with a gesture, and she pauses so kids can answer. | Leave pauses for the child to answer, e.g. "Can you say *volcano*?" |
| **Blippi** | A host with a costume people recognise. He goes on real field trips (fire station, farm, aquarium) and is excited about everything. | Explore real places and objects, with a host or mascot kids recognise. |
| **CoComelon / ChuChu TV / Super Simple Songs** | Bright 3D or 2D animation and short, catchy songs. The same characters appear in every video, and old songs are re-cut into long compilations. They also dub videos into many languages. | Recurring characters, songs, long compilations, and versions in other languages. |
| **SciShow Kids** | One question per video ("Why do cats purr?"), simple experiments, and a friendly host with a puppet sidekick. | Build each video around a question kids actually ask. |
| **Peekaboo Kidz (Dr. Binocs)** | An animated professor answers science questions in 3 to 5 minutes. He comes back in every video and ends with a recap. | An animated "expert" mascot and a fixed episode format. |
| **Nat Geo Kids / Brave Wilderness (kids-friendly cuts)** | Real animal footage with amazing facts. | Animals are the topic kids click most. |
| **Numberblocks / Alphablocks** | The numbers and letters are characters with personalities. | Turn the idea itself into a character. |

### Patterns they all share

1. **One clear learning goal per video.** The title asks a question and the video answers it.
2. **Recurring characters.** Kids come back to see a character they like, not a topic.
3. **Songs and repetition.** One catchy chorus plus a recap at the end helps facts stick.
4. **A fixed format.** The intro, theme song, question, discovery, recap and outro run in the same order every time, so kids know what to expect.
5. **Bright, simple visuals.** Big shapes, high contrast, little text on screen, and an expressive face in the thumbnail.
6. **Long compilations.** Many big channels earn most of their watch time from 30 to 60 minute compilations of short episodes that parents leave playing.
7. **Shorts as a funnel.** A 30 to 60 second "Did you know?" clip sends viewers to the full episode.
8. **Parents trust them.** No scary content, no shouting, no clickbait. Parents pick what plays, and they also follow on Instagram.
9. **Multiple languages.** Dubbing into Spanish, Hindi, Arabic or Portuguese can double your reach. ChuChu TV is the clearest example.
10. **Uploads on a schedule.** Most post 1 to 3 times a week.

## 2. Rules you must follow (YouTube "Made for Kids" / COPPA)

- Content aimed at children **must be marked "Made for Kids"**. If you don't mark it, YouTube can do it for you, and mislabelling can bring FTC fines.
- Made for Kids videos have **no comments, no personalised ads, no notification bell, no memberships, no merch shelf, and no Stories**. They earn less per view than general content, so plan to earn from several sources:
  - ad revenue at high volume, plus long compilations
  - brand sponsorships with toy, book or education brands that are safe for kids
  - your own products: printable worksheets, colouring books, flashcards, apps, merch
  - licensing to YouTube Kids, Netflix and other streaming platforms once you're big
- Children under 13 can't have Instagram accounts, so **the Instagram is really for parents and teachers**. See section 4.
- Never show children's full names, schools or locations. Get written consent before you film any child.

## 3. Content plan

**Age group:** pick one to start. **4 to 8 years** works best for informative content: kids are old enough for "why" questions and still young enough to rewatch.

**Show formats:**

| Series | Length | Example |
|---|---|---|
| "Why?" episodes | 4 to 6 min | Why is the sky blue? Why do we sneeze? |
| Animal Superpowers | 3 to 5 min | Mantis shrimp punch, axolotl regrowth, octopus camouflage |
| Around the World | 5 to 7 min | Food, festivals and animals of one country per episode |
| Try It at Home | 3 to 5 min | Safe kitchen science: baking soda volcano, rainbow milk |
| Learn-a-Song | 2 to 3 min | Planets song, continents song, body parts song |
| Shorts: "Did you know?" | 30 to 60 s | One amazing fact with a visual payoff |
| Compilations | 30 to 60 min | "All Animal Superpowers, Season 1" |

**Episode template:** a 5-second hook question, then the theme song (10 s max), then the mascot tells a story, then the discovery or experiment, then a "Can you remember?" recap quiz where the mascot pauses for answers, then a teaser for the next question.

**Schedule to start:** 1 long video and 3 Shorts a week. After 3 months, cut a compilation from what you've made.

## 4. Instagram strategy (for parents)

- **Reels (main format):** the best 30 to 60 second fact clips from YouTube, plus a "Fact of the day".
- **Carousels:** "5 questions to ask your kid about volcanoes", printable activity previews, conversation cards. Education accounts get strong engagement from carousels.
- **Stories:** polls and quizzes ("Which animal can regrow its heart?"), behind the scenes.
- **Link in bio:** the YouTube channel, plus a free printable to collect parent emails. Your email list is the audience you own.
- **Collaborate** with parenting and homeschool creators and with teachers.
- Keep the same mascot, colours and font on both platforms.

## 5. Name ideas

Criteria: short, easy for a 5-year-old to say, about learning or curiosity, available as a handle, and not too close to an existing brand.

### Top picks

| Name | Handle idea | Why it works | Quick check (web search, 2026-10-10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WhyZoo** | @whyzoo / @whyzookids | "Why" plus "zoo": curious questions and animals, the two biggest draws for kids. Easy to say, and a mascot fits naturally (a zoo of curious animals). | No existing kids' brand found. |
| **Wonder Cubs** | @wondercubs | Cubs are curious little kids, and the cub mascots can grow up as the channel grows. | No kids' channel found. Avoid any Chicago Cubs-style logo. |
| **Curio Club** | @curioclubkids | Short for curiosity. "Club" makes kids feel they belong ("Welcome, Curio Club members!"). | No YouTube channel found. A Vietnamese education company called *CURIOOkids* exists, so the spelling must stay different. |
| **Hoot & Know** | @hootandknow | A wise owl mascot, and "know" sounds like "now". | No match found. *Giggle and Hoot* (ABC Australia) is a preschool show, which is close but different. |
| **Tiny Atlas** | @tinyatlaskids | Fits the "explore the world" format and sounds premium to parents. | No kids' channel found. |

### More options

- **Brainy Burrow**: animal friends live in a burrow and learn underground
- **Little Big Questions**: describes the show, though it's longer
- **Whizzle**: from "whiz", playful and easy to trademark
- **Spark Sprouts**: growing young minds
- **Factopus**: an octopus with 8 arms holding 8 facts. Memorable mascot and name in one.
- **Ask Ollie**: a host or mascot name that doubles as the show name ("Ask Ollie: Why do bees buzz?")
- **KnowZoo**: an alternative to WhyZoo
- **Pocketpedia**: an encyclopedia that fits in your pocket

### My recommendation

**WhyZoo**, with a mascot animal who asks "Why?" in every episode. Each video title becomes a WhyZoo question ("WhyZoo: Why do zebras have stripes?"). This gives you the recurring character, the question format and the animal appeal that the top channels rely on. **Factopus** is a strong second choice if you want the mascot to be the brand.

### Before you lock the name in

1. Check that the handle is free on YouTube, Instagram and TikTok, and that the `.com` domain is free (namecheckr.com or namechk.com check them all at once).
2. Search the trademark databases: USPTO (US), IP India or UKIPO for your country, and WIPO Global Brand Database, in classes 41 (education/entertainment) and 9/16 (videos, books).
3. Say it to a few real 5 to 8 year olds. If they can't repeat it back, pick another name.

# Update (2026-10-10): kindness and good-values channel

The channel's focus is now **good values**: kindness, helping others, sharing, honesty, saying sorry, and making good choices. The aim is for kids to learn something and to keep coming back.

## How the best values shows teach without preaching

| Show | What it does | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| **Daniel Tiger's Neighborhood** | Gives every lesson a short, singable "strategy song" ("When you feel so mad that you want to roar, take a deep breath and count to four") | One song per value that kids can sing in real life |
| **Bluey** | Characters make real mistakes and work them out through play. There is no lecture at the end. | Show the lesson, don't tell it |
| **Sesame Street** | Has a whole neighbourhood of lovable characters, each with their own personality and flaw | Build a world with a cast, not a single host |
| **Peppa Pig / Masha and the Bear** | Small everyday problems (sharing a toy, losing a game) that kids recognise | Use stories from a kid's own day |

## Name ideas

| Name | Idea | Quick web check |
|---|---|---|
| **Kindlings** ⭐ | "Kind" + "kindle" (a little spark). The characters *are* the Kindlings, tiny friends who light up when they do something kind. | No existing kids' show found |
| **Heartville** ⭐ | A town where everyone learns to be kind, like Sesame Street. Pairs well with Kindlings: "The Kindlings of Heartville". | No existing kids' channel found |
| **Kind Kingdom** | A kingdom where kindness is the real superpower | No kids' channel found |
| **Bee Kind Buddies** | Bee mascots: "Bee kind, bee honest, bee helpful" | Common phrase, so the handle may be taken |
| **Goody Grove** | A forest of animal friends who do good deeds | Not checked |
| **Little Kind Heroes** | Kids see helping others as being a hero | Not checked |
| **Helping Paws** | Animal friends who help each other | Name is common for pet charities. Check it first. |
| **Sunny Hearts** | Warm and positive, good for toddlers | Not checked |
| **Good Deed Gang** | Easy to remember | Close to *The Good Deed Crew* book series. Keep it as a backup only. |
| **Kindness Crew** | Clear meaning | Widely used by school clubs, so it's hard to own as a brand. Avoid. |

**Recommendation: "Kindlings"** as the channel name and the name of the characters, with **Heartville** as the town they live in.
- Title format: *"Kindlings: Milo Learns to Share"*
- Catchphrase: *"Little spark, big heart!"*
- Every time a Kindling does something kind, their heart glows. That gives each episode a visual payoff kids wait for.

## What keeps kids coming back

1. **Lovable characters with flaws.** One is shy, one is bossy, one doesn't like sharing, and one tells fibs. Kids love a character who is *learning*, not one who is already perfect.
2. **Mistake, then feeling, then fix.** The character gets it wrong, sees how it makes someone feel, and makes it right. Never shame anyone, and never end with a lecture.
3. **One song per value**, the Daniel Tiger method. Parents will sing it at home ("Sharing is caring, take a turn, then it's mine…").
4. **A recurring signature moment.** For example, the heart glows and the "Kindness Meter" in the town square fills up.
5. **A Kindness Challenge at the end of every video.** "This week, help someone carry something!" Parents can post photos on Instagram, and kids come back to see the next one.
6. **A collectible "Kindness Badge"** for each value. Offer printable badge charts for parents through your Instagram bio link.
7. **A story across episodes.** Fill the town's Kindness Meter to 100 to unlock a festival or a new character. This gives kids a reason to watch the next one.
8. **Same day, same time.** For example, a new episode every Friday.

## First 12 episode ideas

1. Milo Learns to Share (his toy truck)
2. The Broken Vase (telling the truth)
3. Saying Sorry (and meaning it)
4. The New Kid in Heartville (including others)
5. Helping Grandma (helping at home)
6. When I Lose a Game (being a good sport)
7. The Angry Volcano Feeling (calming down instead of hitting)
8. Please and Thank You Day
9. Being Brave Enough to Say No (when friends want to do something wrong)
10. Taking Care of Nature (no littering, being kind to animals)
11. Waiting My Turn (patience)
12. Kindness Festival (the season finale, when the meter reaches 100)

## Format mix

- **Main episodes:** 4 to 7 minute animated stories, one value each
- **Songs:** 1 to 2 minute music video for each value. Songs get replayed the most.
- **Shorts and Reels:** a 30 second "What would you do?" moment. Pause and ask: share or not share? Then show the kind choice.
- **Compilations:** "All Sharing Stories – 30 min", "Kindlings Season 1"
- **Instagram (parents):** the weekly Kindness Challenge, printable reward charts, and "talk about it with your child" carousels
